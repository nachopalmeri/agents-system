# Zero to One: product strategy prompts

Use only for product differentiation or strategy questions. Keep the answer proportional to the user's decision; a short hypothesis and one test can be enough.

## Focus the bet

- **Initial market:** Name a narrow group with a shared painful job, a buyer, and a practical way to reach them. Expand after evidence from this group.
- **Different value:** State why this group would switch: “For [buyer], we improve [outcome] over [current alternative] because [specific advantage].” Make the improvement measurable; AI alone is not an advantage.
- **Alternatives:** Include direct competitors, manual work, spreadsheets, and doing nothing. Check switching effort, existing spending, and why the current solution survives.
- **Distribution:** Name the first channel, how to access it, and a plausible path from contact to paid use. Estimate acquisition effort or cost against expected revenue; separate tested access from a guess.
- **Durability:** Ask what could preserve the advantage as others copy it. Consider workflow integration, earned trust, lawful data access, network effects, or lower costs at scale. Label each as evidence or hypothesis; feature novelty alone proves little.

## Test before expanding

Choose the cheapest experiment that could disprove the riskiest assumption: an interview about past behavior, a manual service, a prototype, or a paid pilot. Stay within existing authorization for outreach, spending, and publishing.

Before testing, record the target group, sample or denominator, deadline, budget, observable commitment, and numeric pass/fail thresholds. Agree on stop, revise, and scale criteria suited to the user's capacity. Avoid moving thresholds after seeing results.

Example only: within two weeks, offer ten qualified buyers a manual pilot; proceed if three pay the proposed price and two return for the next job. Stop or revise if payment falls short; test repeat use before scaling acquisition. These numbers are illustrative, not universal benchmarks. Inconclusive evidence calls for a bounded follow-up, not automatic scaling.

## Source and limits

Adapted strategy prompts inspired by *Zero to One* by Peter Thiel and Blake Masters. The [official Penguin Random House page](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/234730/zero-to-one-by-peter-thiel-with-blake-masters/9780804139304/), verified October 20, 2026, describes creating distinct value rather than copying existing businesses. The validation method and thresholds here are practical adaptations, not book quotations or a claim of full-book coverage.
